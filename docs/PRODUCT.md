# Discourse Graphs panels

## Register

product

## Users

Researchers who already think in Roam or Obsidian. They are reading and writing, and a panel sits beside their notes. They work alone or in a lab, and they move between bases more often than the tools admit.

## Purpose

One set of panels, opened inside each base, so Roam and Obsidian users share a mental model of nodes, relations and canvases. Data stays local first. Sync is optional.

The canvas is first tuned for building an argument: 10 to 30 cards, with every title readable at normal zoom. A paper's extraction is the same size, so a paper arrives as a canvas people can read straight away.

Labs share canvases and papers. Everything shared stays a choice: what goes to the lab, whether the file goes with it, and whether anyone sees that you read it.

## Personality

The panels take each base's look: its fonts, colors and corners, including the person's theme. DG stays recognizable through layout, interaction and node type colors, not through a brand skin.

There is one style: calm and dense. Controls stay out of the way, and color carries meaning only.

## Anti-references

- A web app embedded in a note-taking tool, with its own brand fighting the base
- Whiteboard toys, where decoration competes with the argument
- Dashboards with metrics and cards for their own sake

## Principles

- The same structure and the same gestures in every base. Only the skin changes.
- Fit the frame people really use. Measured: 630 to 900 px beside a sidebar is the common canvas frame, and 300 to 680 px is the side panel.
- Research titles are long. Text wraps and stays readable; nothing truncates a claim on a card.
- Color means something: node type, relation kind or state.
- Nothing irreversible without a way back.
